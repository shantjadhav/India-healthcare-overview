🏗️ Structure of India's Healthcare System
Public Sector

Operated by Central and State governments
Includes Primary Health Centres (PHCs), Community Health Centres (CHCs), District Hospitals
Free or heavily subsidized care but chronically underfunded
India spends ~2.1% of GDP on health (public expenditure), well below the WHO recommendation of 5%

Private Sector

Accounts for ~70–80% of outpatient visits and ~60% of inpatient care
Highly fragmented ranges from solo practitioners to large hospital chains (Apollo, Fortis, Manipal)
Expensive and largely out-of-pocket for most Indians

Out-of-Pocket Expenditure (OOPE)

~47–50% of total health expenditure is paid directly by households
One of the highest OOPE ratios globally
Leading cause of financial catastrophe for low-income families

📋 Major Policy Frameworks
Ayushman Bharat (2018)

World's largest government-funded health insurance scheme
PM-JAY: Covers ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary care
Targets ~500 million beneficiaries (bottom 40% economically)
Health and Wellness Centres (HWCs): Upgraded PHCs for comprehensive primary care

National Health Policy 2017

Target: Increase public health expenditure to 2.5% of GDP by 2025
Focus on preventive and promotive health
Emphasis on universal health coverage

National Digital Health Mission (ABDM)

Launched 2021
Creates Health ID, Digital Health Records, Healthcare Professionals Registry
Foundation for India's healthtech infrastructure

Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY)

Insurance-based coverage for hospitalization
Cashless treatment at empaneled public and private hospitals


🦠 Disease Burden
India faces a dual burden of disease:
Communicable Diseases (still significant)

Tuberculosis — India has the world's highest TB burden (~27% of global cases)
Malaria, Dengue, Typhoid remain prevalent in rural/urban areas
HIV/AIDS — 2.4 million people living with HIV

Non-Communicable Diseases (rapidly rising)

Cardiovascular diseases: leading cause of death (~28% of all deaths)
Diabetes: ~77 million diabetics — 2nd highest globally
Cancer: ~14 lakh new cases per year
Mental health disorders: ~197 million affected; severe shortage of psychiatrists

💡 Opportunities for Healthtech
These gaps are exactly where technology creates value:

Telemedicine — Bridging rural specialist access (e.g., Practo, eSanjeevani)
AI diagnostics — Radiology, pathology, dermatology AI tools for low-resource settings
Digital health records — ABDM infrastructure enabling continuity of care
Preventive health platforms — Chronic disease management apps (diabetes, hypertension)
Supply chain & cold chain tech — Vaccine and drug distribution optimization
Mental health platforms — iCall, YourDOST, Vandrevala Foundation scaling access
Health insurance tech (insurtech) — Simplifying claims, reducing OOPE
